1At a glance

The question: why does Gemma 4 31B, with thinking off, send the same tool call again and again on a coding task, and what breaks the loop?

The loop is a symptom of a state the model is already sure about, and it is held in place by its own copies; removing the loop doesn't recover the task. The study's one-sentence summary (findings.md §1). Its status is interpretation: a reading of the measured and observed results below.

3Terms, in one picture

The paper would keep "tool-call loops", with onset and lock-in (literature.md §2). "Return" is dropped unless a section needs it.

TermMeaning
Run, kConsecutive identical tool calls; k counts the identical calls so far.
Copy · return · staleA copy repeats the call just before; a return repeats an earlier one; stale calls are both.
Loop taskA task run where at least half the calls are bad (stale or malformed).
Lock-in hazardP(one more identical call | the last k calls were identical, the last one well formed).
Clean stepNo budget warning in the last result, and no compaction before the next call.
Onset · lock-inOnset is the first copy after a fresh call (k = 1); lock-in is continuing once copying has started (k ≥ 2).
Escape (one step ahead)A next call that is neither stale nor malformed. Not a solved task.
Control · fixed requestThe model sees its copies (ctrl) or the request after the run's first call at every step (dedup).

7The formal model (proposed 2026-10-24)

One paragraph for §4, about 180 words. It borrows a known result: in survival analysis and econometrics, differences between individuals alone make a hazard rise ("frailty"; "heterogeneity against state dependence"). Our fixed-request proxy separates the two by intervention.

Model
Each state s has a chance ps of drawing the same call again from the fixed request. Under dedup each step is a fresh draw from that request, so
h0(k) = E[pk] / E[pk−1]
Proposition 1: selection alone makes the curve rise (for any mix of states)
h0(k) never falls with k. By Cauchy–Schwarz, E[pk]2 ≤ E[pk−1] · E[pk+1]. So a rising lock-in curve is no evidence of reinforcement on its own: the claim withdrawn on 10-20. Test: the dedup curve must be non-decreasing, and it is (0.78, 0.87, 0.93, 0.99).
Decomposition
Selection is h0(k) − h0(1). Reinforcement is hctrl(k) − h0(k), measurable from k = 3 (dedup − control: −7.0 points at 3–4 and −4.1 at 5–9; none at k = 2).
Sketch: a Beta mix (Fig 6)
With p ~ Beta(a, b):
h0(k) = (a + k − 1) / (a + b + k − 1), a ≈ 0.006, b ≈ 0.26
Two observed points set it; the later bins come out at 0.90, 0.95 and 0.99 against 0.88, 0.93 and 0.99.
Proposition 2: the tail
P(run ≥ k) falls like k−b, and with b < 1 the expected run length E[1/(1 − p)] is infinite: a run has no typical length, so a budget, a compaction or the task's end stops it. That ties finding 4 to finding 2.
Status and cost
Proposition 1 and the decomposition hold whatever the fit shows. The fit is a new analysis (per-step counts from loops.json, maximum likelihood by k, task resamples), about half a day; if it fails, drop the Beta and Proposition 2.

8Limits, and claims withdrawn

What the study can't claim (findings.md §5)

  • Generality: two sizes of one family, thinking off, one harness, four repositories, 109 tasks. The 12B tests the descriptive claims only.
  • Small effects on resolution: one run varies by about ±2.2 tasks; an inconclusive difference is not equivalence.
  • The forced thought over whole tasks: T-open is one step ahead only.
  • Calibrated confidence: we measure re-draw probabilities, not calibration.
  • That escapes are progress, or that the block and dedup are entries: a Kaggle zip can change neither the template nor the requests.
  • Where in the model: nothing looks inside it.
  • Replay validity: v30 histories scored in v31's environment (p(c) 0.639 against the recorded 0.634).

Withdrawn on the way (findings.md §4)

  • The block lowers the stale share 5–6 points → it fragments runs; no drop on v31.
  • Editing the context beats thinking → self-selection: the model skipped thoughts where they would help.
  • Lock-in is self-reinforcement → partly; most of it is selection (G4).
  • The repeated result is the cue → the call and the boundary before it are (G2).
  • Dedup is the strongest one-step lever → G2's version also deleted calls sent before the run.
  • Run-start states have p(c) 0.074 → the closed loop measures 0.78 at k = 2.
  • v31 doubles malformed calls → noise.

9The paper plan (proposed, not agreed)

Three framings

Recommended

A. Mechanism: "Selected, Then Sealed"

Loops start in states where the model would draw the same call almost every time; its own copies close the few escapes left; only the harness ends them; so breaking the loop leaves the task unsolved.

Novelty: the selection–reinforcement split and how loops end are unmeasured elsewhere, and they explain the null. Quality: an instrument any agent study can use, and the same curve at 12B.

B. Measurement: "The Lock-In Hazard"

The hazard by run length, with a fixed-request control, splits a loop into selection and reinforcement.

Rejected as the lead: undersells the story and edges toward the Best New Resource award.

C. Levers: "Breaking the Loop Doesn't Fix the Task"

Large one-step effects reshape the loops over whole tasks, but resolution stays flat.

Rejected as the lead: the null is already reported (SWE-smith, Gumaan, Liu) and can't rule out ±2 tasks.
